Your plan for the task. Ops apply in order; on the first failing op the earlier ones stay and the result names its fix. Write an op as short text, `<op> [item][: text]`, parts split by `|`:
- `init Phase: item | item` (replaces the list), `append: item | item` or `append Phase: item` (a new phase name adds the phase)
- `start 1.3`, `done 1.2` or `done` for the current item (the next pending item starts by itself), `drop 1.2`, `block 1.4: reason`, `unblock 1.4`, `rm 1.5`
- `plan: step | step` (current item) or `plan 2.1: step | step`, `step` (next step) or `step 3`, `closed` (lists closed items)
Address items by number like 2.3 or by text. The object form {op, item, phase, list, items, reason, steps, step} also works. While items are open you see them every turn with the current item's plan.

Every work tool takes the same ops in its optional `todo` field; `custom`, `load_tools` and the GPT patch form of `edit` do not. All calls of a response get their todo ops applied in call order before any call runs, so the list costs no response and no wait. Keep the list there: never spend a response on todo alone while work goes on, put the change into the field of your next work call. When a response holds only tools without the field, call this tool in that same response, next to them; alone only when a change is due and no other call is left.

- Keep a list for every task that changes files or runs several stages of work: init it, in Russian with a named phase, in the `todo` field of your first calls. Skip it for a question answered by reading and searching, for one answer and for one short action.
- Each item is a concrete outcome: what will be true, where, and what shows it is done. When you start an item, give it a plan of concrete steps with the decisions they carry; mark steps as you go. A finding you will need again goes to its own `local://<subject>.md`, named in the item; reread those files after a context loss.
- Extend an unfinished list with append; drop an old list's remaining items before moving to a different task.
- Close the last items in the `todo` field of the calls you expect to be your last, such as the final check: if their results show more work, `start` the item again in your next call. You cannot finish while items are pending or in progress: each is done, dropped or blocked with its reason, and a blocked item means your last message names the blocker and that you wait for the user.
